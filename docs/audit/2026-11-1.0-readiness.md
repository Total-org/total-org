## 1. Verdict

**Not ready for the owner's test build yet. One figure bug comes first (package R1, one PR). After it the build is ready for the owner to test. A second figure bug (R2) can land during the test but must merge before the 1.0 tag.**

Why:
- **Everything builds and every suite passes** (§2). The full e2e passed 78 of 78 on one run.
- **The books agree with themselves.** In 19 of 21 hand-worked scenarios, every screen agreed with the hand figure to the paisa: the bill, GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, TB, P&L, balance sheet, Outstandings, stock and every simple screen (§4).
- **Two scenarios give wrong figures:**
  - **FIG-G-1 (blocks the test build).** A customer pays an advance in Money in. Later a bill is made for them in Make a bill. The bill never uses the advance. So Home, People and the sales list say the bill is unpaid, and Total offers a reminder to a customer who paid in full. Any shop that takes an advance hits this in simple mode; no setting is needed. Where the customer has a GST rate (set in the full app), GSTR-1 Table 11B also stays empty, so the portal's own GSTR-3B draft counts the advance's tax twice.
  - **FIG-B-1 (before the 1.0 tag).** A contractor's bills cross ₹1,00,000 for the year. Total offers TDS on the last bill only (₹250, not ₹1,250). Once that is deducted, the ₹1,000 shortfall on the earlier bills is shown nowhere. It needs TDS turned on and a 194C contractor, which wizard companies don't start with, so it does not hold up a simple-mode test. It is still high: an accountant who uses TDS gets a wrong tax figure.
- **Tagging 1.0 also needs the owner** (§8): the release-signing key must leave the repository secrets (SEC2-1, open since 13 Nov), and an Apple Developer ID is needed for a notarised build (it also closes SEC3-1).

Counts of new or still-open findings: **0 blocker** in the code (SEC2-1, the owner's GitHub setting, blocks the 1.0 *tag*), **2 high**, **5 medium**, **12 low**, plus observations.

## 3. Findings

Severity: **blocker** = must be fixed before the owner tests · **high** = wrong money or tax figure a normal user will hit · **medium** = wrong in a less common case, or a security gap that needs code already on the Mac · **low** = hardening, wording, or rare.

### 3.1 High

#### FIG-G-1 — High — A bill made in simple mode never uses the customer's (or supplier's) advance
- **Where:** `src/shared/simple/intents.ts:959` — a sale's or purchase's `billRefs` is only `{ kind: 'new', name: billName, … }`; `ctx.openCredits` is used only for refunds. The Outstandings engine nets a named credit off a bill only when an `against` ref names it (`src/shared/outstanding.ts`, by design), and 11B is built only from such refs (`src/main/services/gst.ts:908` `extractAdvanceAdjustments`).
- **Reproduce (ran, scenario S11 / S11b):**
  1. Regular company, a customer with an 18 % rate and 7 days to pay.
  2. Money in on 10 Oct: ₹11,800, "Is this for a bill you haven't made yet?" = yes.
  3. Make a bill in November: ₹10,000 + 18 % = ₹11,800, on credit.
  4. App: Outstandings shows ₹11,800 due **and** ₹11,800 advance held. People, Home and the sales list say ₹11,800 unpaid. After 7 days Home lists it overdue and `lateReminders` offers a reminder. GSTR-1 November Table 11B is empty, while October 11A carries ₹10,000 / ₹900 / ₹900.
  5. Hand: the advance pays the bill; nothing is due; November 11B = ₹10,000 / ₹900 / ₹900.
- **Rule:** Form GSTR-1 Table 11B, "Advance amount received in earlier tax period and adjusted against the supplies being shown in this tax period in Table Nos. 4, 5, 6 and 7" (CGST Rules, Forms, cbic-gst.gov.in). Without it the ₹1,800 counted on receipt (s.13(2)) is counted again on the invoice. This part needs a customer with a GST rate: the scenario set the rate on the ledger; a customer made in simple mode has none, and then 11A is empty instead (FIG-G-2). See §6 Q2.
- **Fixed means:** a NEW sale or purchase in simple mode (Make a bill, Record a purchase, Ask Total offers, the hand-off) whose party holds open named credits shows "Use their advance of ₹X" (ticked by default, can be unticked when the advance is for another order). When ticked, the bill takes those credits, oldest first, as `against` refs up to the bill total; "Paid how?" and the `simple_overpaid` check (`intents.ts:966`) work on what is left after the advance; the preview and the save card say "₹11,800 of their advance is used". Outstandings / People / Home / the sales list then show nothing due, no reminder fires, and GSTR-1 11B carries the adjustment at the advance's rate. Tests: S11 / S11b hand figures, a part advance, an unticked advance, a supplier advance, both modes' hand-off, all three languages. Saved bills are unchanged. The full editor keeps the tick (golden ADV-02).

#### FIG-B-1 — High — TDS on a contractor whose year crosses ₹1,00,000 is offered on the last bill only, and the shortfall then disappears
- **Where:** `src/shared/tdsEligibility.ts:309` — `if (!row || eventBase <= 0 || e.entryBasePaise != null) continue`: an event that carries a deduction skips the threshold test, so the earlier, smaller events never become `aggregate_later`. `src/main/services/tds.ts:493` `tdsSuggestion` takes the bill alone as the base; `undeductedCreditsBefore` (`tdsEligibility.ts:357`) counts credits only. `src/main/services/simplePost.ts:681-684` shows only the rehearsed voucher's own row.
- **Reproduce (ran, scenario 7a):** 194C, an individual contractor, FY 2025-26. Four bills of ₹25,000: nothing due (right). A fifth of ₹25,000: the Eligible tab says ₹1,250 (right). The voucher-entry suggestion and the simple Money out hint offer **₹250**. Deduct ₹250: the four earlier bills drop off the Eligible list, and the ₹1,000 shortfall is shown nowhere.
- **Rule:** the scenario is FY 2025-26, so the Income-tax Act 1961 s.194C(1) and (5). The app's own record of the text is a fragment: "does not exceed thirty thousand rupees ... aggregate ... exceeds one lakh rupees" (`src/main/db/migrations.ts:819`, source https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-194c). The full proviso could not be fetched here (that site answers 403 to scripts). From FY 2026-27 the app models the Income-tax Act 2025 s.393(1) Table Sl. 6(i) with the same limits (`migrations.ts:787`), also not fetched. See §6 Q1: ₹1,250 is due on the usual reading; a CA must confirm the text.
- **Fixed means:** the walk treats a deducted crossing event like an undeducted one for the aggregate test and marks the earlier events `aggregate_later`; each stays eligible until a deduction covers it. The suggestion on the crossing bill or payment offers ₹1,250 (or says "plus ₹1,000 on 4 earlier bills", as the payment banner words it). The simple hint says the whole amount. Tests: 7a–7c turn green; a ₹1,250 deduction clears the list; books where every sum was deducted at the time are unchanged. No ledger or return figure moves; the Eligible list and the suggestion change (before / after table in the PR).

### 3.2 Medium

#### FIG-G-2 — Medium — An advance from a customer without a GST rate is left out of GSTR-1 Table 11A, with no warning
- **Where:** `src/main/services/gst.ts:897` — `extractAdvances` reads only parties with `gst_rate IS NOT NULL`. Every customer the simple New account dialog makes has no rate (`src/renderer/src/simple/NewAccountDialog.tsx:156`). `gstValidate` has no check for it.
- **Reproduce (ran, S11c):** a services business; a new customer made in simple mode pays ₹11,800 as an advance. October 11A is empty; no warning.
- **Rule:** s.13(2) makes the advance for a service taxable on receipt. Rule 50 proviso: "where at the time of receipt of advance,- (i) the rate of tax is not determinable, the tax shall be paid at the rate of eighteen per cent." For goods no tax is due (Notification 66/2017-CT; the app's `advance_goods` warning covers that side). See §6 Q4.
- **Fixed means:** `gstValidate` warns (GSTR-1 checks and the simple Taxes step 1) about advances received in the period, while the registration is regular, from a party with no GST rate, with a new CA point. It does not post or report 18 % by itself: a missing ledger rate is not the same as "not determinable" (a customer of exempt services owes nothing). No simple screen sets a customer's GST rate, so the simple words name the next step: "ask your CA; your accountant sets the customer's GST rate in the full app". A per-receipt "goods or a service, at what rate" answer stays the to-do it already is (CLAUDE.md, advances for goods). Test: S11c gives the warning; a rated party, an unregistered company and a goods-only customer (the `advance_goods` rule) do not.

#### COR-3 (still open) — Medium — A rounded-down bill reports its unrounded total in GSTR-1 `val` and the e-invoice `TotInvVal`
- **Where:** golden B2B-06, `src/main/services/gstGolden/fixtures.ts:61` (`knownBug`, ran: still expected-fail). to-do §2 #207 (a).
- **Proof:** bill ₹101 + ₹9.09 + ₹9.09 = ₹119.18, rounded to ₹119.00. GSTR-1 `val` and e-invoice `TotInvVal` say ₹119.18 with `RndOffAmt` 0. The printed bill says ₹119.00. An IRN whose value differs from the printed invoice is the worst place for this.
- **Recommendation (the second audit's Q3):** report the printed (rounded) total, with `RndOffAmt` −0.18. Reason: the e-invoice schema carries the round off as its own field, and the invoice the buyer holds is the rounded one.
- **This needs the owner's sign-off, not this review's** (§8): it changes filed months. For rounded-down bills `val` and `TotInvVal` move, and the invoice value also decides B2CL against B2CS (`src/shared/gst/returns.ts:124` `b2clLimitOn`; `validate.ts:270`), so an inter-state B2C bill rounded down to the limit (₹1,00,000.40 → ₹1,00,000.00) moves from B2CL to B2CS. Tax and taxable values do not move. The PR carries `figures-approved`, an upgrade-diff table and the CA point.
- **Fixed means:** B2B-06's `knownBug` is removed and passes; a hand-worked case at the B2CL limit; the upgrade test lists only `val` / `TotInvVal` changes and the boundary reclassification.

#### SEC3-1 — Medium — The shipped Mac app loads any library given in `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES`
- **Where:** `package.json` `build.mac` has no `hardenedRuntime`, entitlements or notarisation; `release.yml:106` `CSC_IDENTITY_AUTO_DISCOVERY: 'false'`. `codesign -dv` (ran): `Signature=adhoc`, flags `0x2(adhoc)`, no runtime flag.
- **Proof (ran):** a marker library injected with `DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES` into the packaged app (scratch data dir) ran in the main process and three helpers. This gets around the debug-switch guard (SEC2-3): same-user malware runs code inside Total and can open a business whose passphrase is "remembered on this computer".
- **Fixed means:** Developer ID signature, `hardenedRuntime: true`, notarised, and NO `com.apple.security.cs.allow-dyld-environment-variables` entitlement. Add the same probe beside `debug-switch-check.mjs` in `release.yml` (mac); it must leave the marker file empty. Needs the owner's Apple Developer ID (§8).

#### SEC2-6 (still open) — Medium — "Remember on this computer" lets anyone get past the screen lock by switching business and back
- **Where:** `src/main/ipc.ts:304` `closeCurrentCompany` sets `privacyLock = null`; the reopen tries the remembered key first. The Lock and safety text says the opposite: `src/shared/i18n/catalogues/en.ts:3323` "… The screen lock still works as before."
- **Also:** the privacy lock lives only in memory (`ipc.ts:125`, "a restart opens unlocked"), so quitting and starting Total again gets past it the same way.
- **Decision (owner question Q6, decided here):** a privacy lock of a business whose passphrase is on is STORED outside the books (app level, beside `app-settings.json`, by slug), and while it is set every open of that business asks for the passphrase, remembered or not; only a correct passphrase clears it. Reason: a lock that "Switch business" or a restart removes is not a lock, and the app's own words promise it works. A business without the passphrase lock keeps today's privacy screen (it has no secret to ask for), and the text says so.
- **Fixed means:** dbtests: privacy lock → `company:close` → `company:open` without a passphrase is refused `passphrase_required`; the same after a simulated restart (state re-read from disk); with the passphrase it opens and the stored lock is cleared; without a privacy lock the remembered key still opens it. The Lock and safety sentence says exactly this in all three languages.

#### UI-1 — Medium — The Gateway's GST card pairs one month's period with the next month's GSTR-1 date, so an overdue GSTR-1 never shows
- **Where:** `src/main/services/dashboard.ts:110-132` — `gstr1Due` is the first GSTR-1 deadline in the next 45 days, while the period is the month before the next GSTR-3B.
- **Proof (ran, sample books on 18 Nov):** the Compliance card says "GST returns · Oct 2026 — GSTR-1 in 23 days". That is November's GSTR-1 (11 Dec). October's GSTR-1 was due 11 Nov and is a week late; nothing says so.
- **Fixed means:** the card shows the GSTR-1 date of the period it names, and a passed date reads as late (as the plain Taxes screen already does: `TaxesScreen.tsx:211`, "this date has passed"). Test: on 18 Nov with October not marked filed, the card says October's GSTR-1 date has passed.

## 5. Security and data safety (ran / read)

**Checked and fine:**
- **Restore** (read, not run): the chosen file is validated (quick_check, company row, not newer) before a `pre-restore` copy is taken and the swap runs (copy → fsync → rename → folder fsync); a failed swap reopens the old books. A newer file is refused on every way in (open, restore, encrypted import, unknown `TOTALBK<n>`).
- **Before a migration on open** a checked copy is taken; no copy, no migration. A restore migrates without one (`ipc.ts:1087`, `preUpgradeCopy: false`): the backup file it came from stays in the backups folder and a pre-restore snapshot was taken.
